# Decoding failures, decoder classes and active attacks

For HQC, actual decryption error is `z=Truncate(x·r₂+r₁·y+e,ℓ)` over `F₂`, not any one sampled support. Its RMRS decoder uses an outer shortened Reed–Solomon code and duplicated inner Reed–Muller code; the 2025 specification claims DFR below `2^-128`, `2^-192`, `2^-256` for the sets. Its Section 6.1 analyzes the error distribution and decoder, and NIST IR 8545 explicitly notes a **coefficient-independence simplifying assumption** in the DFR estimate. A modeled tail is not an unconditional bound over each key/message/adversarial ciphertext. The salt and new 2025 salted FO transform address multi-ciphertext and rejection concerns; removing them changes the reduction.

For BIKE, error `(e₀,e₁)` of total weight `t` is recovered from `c₀h₀=e₀h₀+e₁h₁`. Iterative bit-flipping thresholds and convergence depend on the sparse private Tanner graph. Average DFR `E_key P(fail|key)` can hide rare weak keys with high `P(fail|key)`. NIST's [Round-4 report](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2025/NIST.IR.8545.pdf) documents gathering weak keys, near codewords and an earlier configuration's `≥2^-117` average DFR contribution, plus the v5.2/BIKE-flip decoder response; it did **not** establish full tail certainty for every secret. [Arpin et al.'s 2025 Markov model](https://www.nist.gov/publications/error-floor-prediction-markov-models-qc-mdpc-codes) predicts `<2^-129.5` for a **modified** level-1 block length 13477, rather than the submitted 12323, subject to its model. The [2026 Annechini et al. paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1043) develops a closed-form model for a specified QC-MDPC iterative decoder and claims much shorter objects versus HQC; its abstract calls this an estimate/model, so it is not a license to equate average and worst-key failure or to claim its approach novel here.

| Decoder | Redundancy / helper | Failure profile | Constant-time and attack concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public concatenated RMRS (HQC) | Long, very low-rate public codeword `v` | Modelable minimum-distance/inner-tail behavior; correlation assumption remains | Fixed-iteration public decoder possible; implementation leakage from branches/table accesses remains |
| Private QC-MDPC bit-flipping (BIKE) | Short syndrome and hidden sparse parity checks | Key-dependent graph traps, near-codeword error floors, thresholds | Fixed iteration needed; convergence/iteration and timing leakage can reveal checks |
| QC-LDPC iterative decoding | Even sparser graph | May decode well, but structure/key recovery and error floors | Sparse dual search and reaction attacks first |
| Syndrome decoding with Goppa trapdoor (Classic McEliece) | Huge public matrix, short syndrome | Perfect correctness for valid encapsulations per NIST | Key-generation/storage cost; side-channel implementation still matters |
| List/soft decoding | Potential radius improvement at higher runtime | Ambiguity/list selection must be bounded and authenticated | Data-dependent work and candidate lists threaten constant time and CCA behavior |

Invalid inputs can be malformed ciphertexts or chosen syndromes; valid-input average DFR does not quantify adversarial failure observations. A public accept/reject signal, timing, branch, memory/cache state, decoder iteration count or convergence trace can act as a reaction oracle. FO implicit rejection limits explicit feedback only if its implementation is constant time and never leaks these distinctions. Any new representation must reject duplicate/out-of-range supports, noncanonical padding, truncated syndromes and equivalent encodings **before** a canonical re-encryption comparison, with all secret-dependent behavior hidden. No rare-event experiment in this phase establishes a per-key `2^-x` bound.
