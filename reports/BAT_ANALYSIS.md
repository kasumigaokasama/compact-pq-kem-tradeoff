# BAT baseline: what can and cannot be reconstructed

The primary ePrint abstract describes an NTRU KEM whose secret is a short basis, not just a short vector. With one sign convention the basis contains `f,g,F,G` satisfying `gF-fG=q`, and the public key is an NTRU ratio. The second relation gives a second equation in message and error, allowing a decoder with a smaller modulus. This describes a **mechanism**, not an independently reproduced full set of algorithms. The exact code/sampler/CCA transform must be read in the pinned corrected paper.

The Phase 2A comparison reports BAT-512 public key **521 B** and ciphertext **473 B**. A historical accounting model takes `n=512, q=257`: one unrestricted polynomial has `512·log₂(257)≈4098.88` information bits, whose fixed-byte minimum is 513 B. A 521-B published key therefore includes formatting/packing overhead or a different admissible alphabet; without the canonical codec it cannot be attributed more precisely. An illustrative ciphertext ledger, from Phase 2A's historical source, is a 456-byte packed polynomial plus 16-byte auxiliary and 1-byte tag = 473 bytes. **Neither byte layout has been independently matched to author test vectors here.**

The comparison is valuable because a short basis enables different decoding and modulus choices, but key generation is substantially more complex than simple ratio sampling. The 2026 proof correction is a blocker for relying on the historical reduction. BAT remains a useful **size and mechanism research comparator**; proof confidence and specific security bounds are unclassified until `BAT_PROOF_DIFF.md` is completed.
